LeakLocate · the problem

Cities lose a quarter of their treated water to leaks, and the small leaks hide inside normal swings in demand

  • 25–30 % of treated drinking water is lost before it reaches a tap; above 50 % in many cities (World Bank estimates)
  • Small, continuous leaks are most of that loss and run for months before anyone sees water on the surface

The city’s questions

  1. Limit water loss: how many pressure sensors should we install, and where? Part 1 · sensor plan
  2. Limit time to fix: when pressures change, which streets should the crew search first? Part 2 · find the leak
  3. Trust: how sure is that search area, and when should we not rely on it? What’s new
Sources: World Bank non-revenue water estimates via Aquatech; flow of EPANET’s Net3 benchmark network from this project’s simulation

LeakLocate

Answer: plan sensors per city by simulation, then locate leaks with a forest × physics hybrid whose search areas are small and reliable.

  • Part 1 · sensor plan: every junction of six city networks simulated as a leak; sensors added where they cut the search distance most; a recommended count per city
  • Part 2 · find the leak: a random forest fused with a hydraulic physics model points the crew to a small search area
  • Reliable: an 80 % search area contains the leak in about 80 % of cases, measured on held-out leaks
  • Judged fairly: a benchmark ladder from best-guess baselines upward, 24,000 EPANET simulations

Current best → this work

All results are simulation on the EPANET Net3 benchmark (92 junctions). Held-out test set: 4,000 leaks from unseen random seeds, 0.5–16 L/s, 6 sensors, 5 % demand noise.

Limits today

  • Simulation only, on a 92-junction benchmark.
  • Wrong pipe data is handled up to about 20 % error, and only with a leak-free baseline period.
  • One leak at a time; no sensor faults.
  • Small leaks (under 2 L/s) stay close to the noise floor.

What if this were a company

Product: turn a district-level leak alert into a ranked, street-level search area

1 · Detect

Is something leaking?

Flow balance per district, night-flow analytics.

Today: utility SCADA, analytics platforms such as TaKaDu

2 · Narrow · LeakLocate

Which streets?

Pressure loggers + the hybrid locator → a ranked search area with calibrated coverage; plus where to put the next logger.

Gap today: crews sweep whole districts

3 · Pinpoint

Which metre?

Acoustic correlators and ground microphones, accurate to about 1 m.

Today: Gutermann, FIDO, field crews

4 · Repair

Dig and fix

Work orders, excavation, verification.

Today: utility or contractor crews

Buyer

  • Small and mid-size utilities with district metering, losses above 20 %, and no in-house data team.
  • NRW contractors paid on water saved.

Possible partners

  • Pressure-logger makers (bundle the software with loggers).
  • Acoustic service firms (we shrink their search area).
  • Engineering consultancies that own utilities’ hydraulic models.
  • Development banks funding performance-based NRW contracts.

Why it could win

  • Uses the hydraulic model and loggers a utility already has.
  • Search regions whose stated coverage is measured and holds (81 % for a nominal 80 %).
  • Tells the utility where the next logger pays most.
Names are illustrative market players, not partners. Sources: TaKaDu, Gutermann, FIDO Tech, ASTERRA public sites

1–2 pilot districts with a utility partner; back-test on past repairs; calibrate on SCADA. Gate: crews search measurably less pipe.

6–18 months · productize

Hosted platform; logger bundle with one hardware partner; first paid contracts, priced per district. Gate: 10 paying utilities, renewal.

18–36 months · scale

Performance-based contracts with NRW contractors and development banks; GNN models; more network types. Gate: payback under 12 months for buyers.

Three ways to charge

  • Per district per year (software + logger plan).
  • Share of verified savings in performance-based NRW contracts.
  • Sensor-plan studies for consultancies, one-off.
